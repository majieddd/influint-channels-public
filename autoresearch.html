The loop that tries to widen what this system can measure, and a record of what it was thinking while it did. Each iteration takes one claim the ledger cannot currently grade, proposes a single measurement for it, and code scores that proposal across every channel in the knowledge base. Nothing here changes a standing, a claim or the ledger: a kept proposal is a proposal, and the lever audit is still the gate.

The metric it is trying to move

The measurement frontier: how many live claims the ledger can actually grade. It rises only when a proposal reproduces across independent channels at the same bar a discovery has to clear, which is why it moves slowly and why it cannot be moved by loosening anything — the thresholds live in code the loop is not allowed to edit.

A gap the vocabulary could not reach, and the probe built for it

One claim in the queue below counts independent creators, and no per-video accessor can express that: a figure about one video inside one channel cannot see across creators at all, and the loop said so rather than forcing a match. It has its own probe now, over the niche crawl instead of the channel store, and its unit is a niche rather than a channel. Shapes are mined from titles by the same statistics the format library uses, each creator is hidden in turn, and the question is whether a shape that worked for others also worked for them. Every figure is corrected against a shuffled null, because prediction improves with more evidence under pure noise. That correction is not enough on its own, and an adversarial audit proved it: shuffling destroys the differences between shapes, so the null cannot see an artefact that rides on them. The screen deciding which trials are asked therefore reads a FIXED number of creators whatever the evidence size, measured at 3% false positives on corpora where the claim is false by construction, down from 13% before.

Each niche could have detected an effect of about 0.17, so an effect that size or larger is unlikely to be there. A smaller one would not have shown up here, and this cannot rule it out.

AVD and APV normally move inversely (longer videos buy duration at the cost of percentage). When BOTH rise in the same window, that is the specific signature of genuine retention improvement, and it is the condition that licenses lengthening videos.

Declined. The first half of this claim is an identity, not an empirical relationship: average percentage viewed IS average view duration divided by runtime, so 'AVD and APV move inversely as videos get longer' is arithmetic rather than a finding, and every accessor that could express it (runtime, avd-sec, apv) shares the runtime series with the others.
