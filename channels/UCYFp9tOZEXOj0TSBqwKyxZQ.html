Formats

The repeatable shapes this channel publishes, written as templates. A format is a shape a viewer recognises as “another one of those” — so two videos belong together when they make the same promise, even if they share no words. Ordered by how well the shape performs against the channel’s own median, not by how often it is used: a format shipped twenty times that underperforms is a habit, not a strength. The multiplier is the group’s median outlier score, measured against each video’s contemporaries. A shape marked era-bound ran in a block — most of its neighbouring uploads share the same shape, so its score compares it against itself and cannot be read as performance. Interleaving two formats over the same months is what makes them comparable.

Colour

Median band is the view to start from on a large catalogue. A hundred and sixty curves on one pair of axes composite into a solid mat that shows only that the channel has a lot of videos; the band draws the same data as percentile envelopes, so the middle half of the catalogue and the full 10th-to-90th spread are readable at a glance. Percentiles rather than mean and standard deviation, because retention curves are not symmetric and a couple of duds would drag a mean band below where most videos actually live. Click any title to draw that one video in gold against the envelope, which answers whether it sits inside the pack or outside it. All curves is the honest full picture and the right view once a filter has cut the set down.

% of video normalises length, so structural habits line up — a sag every video shares shows as one shared dip. Clock time lets each curve stop where its video stops, so length and endurance become visible. On the % axis the dashed white line is this channel's own median — the bar every video is judged against; clock time has no shared x, so no median is drawn. By upload date adds a third axis: each plane is one slice of the channel’s history, receding by when it was published — oldest at the back. Each plane draws the MEDIAN curve of the uploads in its window, not every video: a hundred planes land a pixel apart and stack into a solid wall, and the question is whether retention drifted, not what one video did. Median rather than mean, so a single breakout cannot speak for a whole quarter. Window narrows the set: the last N uploads holds the sample size constant and answers “has my recent work changed”, while the last N days answers “what has this channel been doing lately” and lets a quiet stretch show up as a near-empty chart rather than as a flattering handful of curves. Length and Views slide over PERCENTILES of the catalogue rather than raw numbers, because views are heavy-tailed and a linear slider would spend nine tenths of its travel between the pack and one breakout; the label always shows the real threshold. Scroll to zoom anywhere on the chart and drag to pan — the axes relabel themselves because zooming narrows the DATA shown rather than magnifying the picture, so the type and the curves stay the same weight. Double-click, or Reset, to go back.

What the colours can encode. Recency, views, outlier score and retention at the halfway mark come straight off the record. Average view duration and watch time are DERIVED from the stored curve — its mean is the average share watched, so multiplying by runtime gives the duration and multiplying that by views gives the hours — which means they will not match Studio to the second, because the stored curve is sampled once per percent. A video the chosen metric was never measured for is drawn grey rather than mid-ramp, and the ramp says how many. There is deliberately no returning-viewer encoding: that split is not fetched by any run here, and an option that quietly ranked on something else would be worse than its absence.

Compare draws a reference curve against this channel's own: the median retention curve of every tracked channel, and of the channels in the same confirmed niche once ten or more of them carry curves. Each channel contributes ONE curve (its own median), so a channel with six hundred analysed uploads counts the same as one with twelve, and the line is a statement about the fleet rather than about its largest member. Reference curves live on the % axis only, like the channel median, because clock time has no shared x. The education reference is withheld: 1 of 10 channels with curves.
