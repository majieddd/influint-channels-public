Standing is a ladder, and it moves in both directions

A claim's standing answers ONE question: how well is the cause and effect behind it quantified? A gut feeling is a Theory however obviously true it sounds. Measure the lever behind it and it becomes a Candidate Law. Reproduce that across independent channels and have a strategist sign it, and it becomes a Law. Every analysis run re-tests every measurable claim — so a Law whose evidence decays falls back to Candidate Law and has to be re-signed. Nothing keeps its standing because it was true once.

Two things ride alongside standing rather than replacing it. Contested marks a live disagreement — a disputed Candidate Law is still a Candidate Law. And a Rejected claim records which of three ways it died: our own measurements pointed the other way, a strategist overruled it, or it was merged into another claim.

Grading each claim alone is necessary and not sufficient. One measurement, several claims

3 claims, one test named-sponsor-opener-vs-ad-loss · all predicting up
  • The explicit sponsor callout phrase itself causes the drop, not the ad content: naming the sponsor up front is consistently followed by a retention decline, and disclosing AFTER demonstrating the product (framed as gratitude) avoids it.
  • The phrase 'thanks to our sponsor' plus the brand name is itself the skip trigger; showing the product solving the problem FIRST and naming the sponsor only at the end of the segment reduces the drop.
  • Saying the sponsor's name at the START of an integration is itself the trigger for the retention dip — it is an auditory cue that tells viewers exactly when to skip. Moving the words 'this video is sponsored by' to the END of the segment removes the cue.
