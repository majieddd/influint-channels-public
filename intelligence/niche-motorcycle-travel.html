Refuted ideation · checked across 7 channels
Travel footage should be cut as informational/travel-guide content rather than as documentation of the trip — the informational framing gives the video a reason to be found and rewatched.
Source: internal strategy session
Evidence and caveats →

Tested — contradicted: Informational framing UNDER-performs: WITH n=37 median 0.95x vs 1.00x (MWU z=-1.97, p=0.049); age-adjusted 0.87x vs 1.00x (z=-1.88, p=0.061). 5 of the 7 channels with >=2 guide videos came out behind — FortNine 0.40x vs 1.11x, Moto Camp Nerd 0.59x vs 1.08x, MotoJitsu 0.71x vs 1.00x, RevZilla 0.88x vs 1.00x, Chaseontwowheels 0.77x vs 1.03x. Narrative framing sits at or slightly above normal: 1.03x raw, 1.10x age-adjusted (n=9, ns). Broader corpus how-to check across all 46 channels: 0.95x raw / 0.89x age-adjusted, ahead on only 1 of 6 channels.

Caveat: Title framing is a weak stand-in for EDIT framing — a trip cut as a travel guide may still be titled narratively, which would put it in my 'narrative' bucket and blunt the test. The claim also asserts a rewatch benefit, which lifetime views cannot see. The effect is modest and borderline-significant; treat it as 'guide-titled travel content does not outperform' rather than 'informational cutting is wrong'.

They plan to create travel-style, informational content from this trip rather than just documentation, aiming to produce at least three videos from the journey.

Tested: Named event or venue: n=5 across 4 channels, median 1.00x vs 1.00x, z=-0.14, p=0.89. Reactive construction: n=2 — no test possible. Caveat: A null on n=5 is not evidence against the claim, it is an absent sample. Note also the adjacent warning claim (693b8db93f38) that vague reactive phrasing was rewarded and then punished by the algorithm — if both are true the effect is time-dependent, which our single-snapshot corpus could not detect even with a decent sample.

production

Theory production
A motorcycle travel channel should amortize one trip into six distinct videos with different promises — route story, packing, gear test, campsite ranking, camera workflow, and postmortem — rather than one long vlog.
Source: idea-engine research dossier
What would settle it

Does this footage support SIX DISTINCT PROMISES, or one promise cut into six pieces? Khare is right about PARTS — a part-two inherits a weakened package and asks the viewer to have seen part one. The packet's six are not parts: 'packing' and 'campsite ranking' are separate questions with separate titles, separate thumbnails and partly separate audiences, and none of them requires the others. So the rule is not about count at all, it is about whether each upload has an independent, clickable promise that stands alone. The store also supplies the failure mode at the other extreme: Channel I's c112e6452da9 — 30 daily uploads bought a subscriber bump then flatlined by day 30 as reach fell back to the core (~125k average down to 30-40k), which is what happens when the pieces have no independent promise. our strategist's 5e3bd00115b5 (2-4 long-form from a month-long trip) sits between the two and is the operating compromise.
