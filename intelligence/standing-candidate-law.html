Source: Alan Garnett
Reconciled with a conflicting claim

Another claim in this base says something incompatible, and it is not a contradiction — the two apply to different kinds of channel. The question that separates them:

WHERE ON THE EXPOSURE CURVE is this format for this audience? Gannett already holds the reconciling pattern (115f4acc5069): liking follows an inverted-U against exposure count, rising with repetition to a peak and falling after, so a format has a finite number of uses. Both claims are true, on opposite sides of the peak — which converts an argument into a measurement problem: what is the instalment count, and is per-instalment performance still rising? Our mined-format table is weakly consistent with the decay side and worth watching rather than citing: the highest observed lifts sit on small match counts (I Built My Own After Getting Screwed, n=4, 2.92x; Exposing N Secrets, n=3, 2.76x) while the largest cohort in the table (Product Full Review, n=16) sits at 1.67x. That is confounded by naming granularity and cannot carry weight yet, but the store should record instalment index alongside every format so the peak becomes observable rather than argued about.
