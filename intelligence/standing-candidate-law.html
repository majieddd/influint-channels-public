Supported: The largest ABSOLUTE per-sample drop falls inside the first 5% of runtime for 116 of 119 videos (97%). The largest HAZARD RATE falls in the first 5% for 89 of 119 (75%), with the runner-up bucket being the final 5% (9 videos). Our resolution cannot distinguish frame 1 from second 30, so what is confirmed is that the opening BLOCK carries by far the biggest single loss — the claim's prescriptive half (that the first frame must deliver the thumbnail's promise) is untestable here since we do not analyse thumbnails or frames.

Candidate Law analytics · checked across 6 channels Contested
Within a channel, videos with the HIGHEST view counts systematically sit BELOW the channel's average retention, because wider reach pulls in newer, less sticky viewers — so falling retention on a breakout is expected, not a defect.
Source: internal strategy session
What would settle it

Is the video's retention high in ABSOLUTE terms, or high as a RESIDUAL — above what the channel's own retention-versus-views relationship predicts at that view count? Only the residual carries information; the absolute number is confounded by exactly the mechanism claim B names. OUR DATA SETTLES THIS: rank correlation between retention-at-50% and first-28d views is -0.45 (Channel C), -0.51 (Channel D), -0.85 (Channel E), -0.47 (Channel A), -0.89 (Channel F), +0.07 (Channel G) — negative on 5 of 6 channels, strongly so on the two small-n maker channels. The repackaging rule must therefore be re-specified as 'retention above the channel's fitted expectation at that view count', and the existing verdict table in our own pipeline should be checked against this.

Evidence and caveats →
Not measurable by anything we currently compute

No test in our battery measures this claim's variables, so it has never been quantified — which is exactly why it sits at Theory rather than higher. Our probes run over retention curves, runtime, publish dates, view counts, titles and detected intro / sponsor / payoff / outro segments. Claims about click-through rate, impressions, thumbnails, subscriber behaviour, revenue or a viewer's internal motive are outside that reach.

Binding it to a loosely-related test would be worse than leaving it unmeasured: it would attach a real number to the wrong question, and that number would move this claim's standing.
