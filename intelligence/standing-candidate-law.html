Candidate Law ideation · checked across 46 channels
Demand evidence from competitors requires at least five independent examples sharing the same viewer promise but differing in creator, execution and upload date — one outlier is luck.
Source: idea-engine research dossier
Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: Only 11 of 57 stored formats reach 5+ scored evidence videos. 22 of 57 have all evidence from a single channel, so they fail the independence test outright. The formats that fail the bar are precisely the ones carrying the headline numbers: of the 16 formats reporting >=2x lift, 12 rest on <=3 examples and 6 are single-channel. Spearman(distinct channels, lift) = +0.10 — channel diversity buys essentially no lift, meaning a format's high number is not coming from breadth of evidence. And the thin high-lift formats do not survive their own evidence: dropping one instance takes the >=2x group from 2.31x to 1.82x.

Caveat: We can show that thin, single-creator evidence produces unstable lift estimates; we cannot show that a 5-example bar would have produced better-performing ideas, because no ideas were published under both rules. The specific number five is not itself validated — three independent channels would likely capture most of the gain.

Collect at least five examples that share a viewer promise but differ in creator, execution, and upload date.

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: Removing one video — the single best instance — drops a format's median lift by 0.24x (mean 0.34x). Candidate Law packaging · checked across 6 channels
Click-through rate is worth roughly 80% of the value of a successful video and retention the remaining 20%; a video with excellent retention and weak packaging will simply never accumulate impressions, so packaging must be solved before content quality.
Source: Ryan Trahan
What would settle it

Is retention being treated as a CAUSE of reach or as a SYMPTOM of audience narrowness? Our own data forces this question: retention-at-50% is NEGATIVELY rank-correlated with first-28d views on five of six channels we hold curves for (Channel C -0.45, Channel D -0.51, Channel E -0.85, Channel A -0.47, Channel F -0.89; Channel G +0.07). The videos that hold best are systematically the ones that reached fewest people. That is exactly what the the team audits describe from the other side (a85bd294e8c3, cc661b69dba9): reach expands the impression pool faster than click rate or hold rate can survive it, so a top video's low CTR and low retention are marks of broad distribution, not of weak packaging. The correct scoping is therefore: retention is a FLOOR (below the platform's bar, no packaging fixes anything — B is right), not a LEVER (above that floor, more retention buys no additional reach and packaging carries the outcome — A is right). Note our data cannot test the CTR half of either claim; the brief states CTR is not exposed by the API we use.

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: Retention-at-50% vs first-28d views: Channel F -0.89 (n=10, p<0.001), Channel E -0.85 (n=9, p<0.001), Channel D -0.51 (n=30, p=0.002), Channel A -0.47 (n=30, p=0.005), Channel C -0.45 (n=30, p=0.008), Channel G +0.07 (n=10, p=0.85). Controlling duration barely moves them (-0.88, -0.85, -0.50, -0.42, -0.42, +0.01). Duration-free clock marks agree: at 60 seconds the correlations are -0.93, -0.23, -0.16, -0.33, -0.19, -0.66. In 5 of 6 channels retention buys nothing and predicts LESS reach.

Caveat: This supports the ORDERING (packaging dominates retention as a driver of views) but NOT the 80/20 numbers, which are untestable without CTR. And the correlation is causally ambiguous in a way that matters: a video that reaches a broad browse audience is watched by less-qualified viewers, so wide reach probably CAUSES lower retention rather than low retention causing wide reach. Under that reading the negative correlation is a reach artefact and says nothing about the counterfactual value of improving retention on a fixed audience. What it does establish firmly is that within these channels retention is not a route to views.

Candidate Law retention · checked across 7 channels
The standard fix for weak retention — cut the video shorter — is the wrong remedy; a retention problem in the back half is a setup problem in the opening, so the fix is better setup, not less runtime.
Source: Hayden Hillier-Smith
What would settle it

Is the surface LOOP-SCORED (short-form, where completion and rewatch drive redistribution and the final second gates the loop) or watch-time-scored long-form? And is the trim removing a specifically DEAD trailing second, or removing runtime generally? Restated: (a) on short-form, trimming dead trailing frames is a direct lever on the loop metric; (b) on long-form, runtime is not the constraint — our own data has the longer half of the catalogue beating the shorter half on median first-28d views for 6 of 7 channels.

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: The longer half beat the shorter half on median views in 6 of 7 channels (Channel C 321,662 vs 291,465; Channel D 65,205 vs 48,143; Channel A 251,961 vs 218,645; Channel F 1,012,676 vs 963,861; Channel B 287,063 vs 207,873; Channel G 485,039 vs 367,785). Channel E is the sole exception (691,316 vs 863,878). Pooled within-channel rho(duration, views) = +0.23, and rho(duration, AVD-seconds) = +0.69 to +0.85 in every channel — so shortening would directly destroy watch-time seconds while, in these channels, moving views the wrong way.

Caveat: Strictly this refutes only the remedy's premise (that shorter is better), not the claim's positive diagnosis (that the back-half problem originates in the opening) — that half is a causal story we cannot test without an edit experiment. Duration is also confounded with format: a channel's longest videos are often its most ambitious builds, which may attract more views for reasons unrelated to length. Channel E, the longest-form channel at 52-min median, is the one where longer loses.

if you have an act free problem, you have an act One problem. So what is our Act One problem? The common misconception of retention is to make it shorter. Instead, we need to tell it better.

Candidate Law retention · checked across 6 channels
A retention peak is not automatically a good premise — a peak caused by viewers skipping to a timestamp is different from a loved moment, and the discriminator is whether viewers keep watching after it.
Source: idea-engine research dossier
Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: Of 756 rises, only 62.2% (470) were still held two samples later, and 25.1% (190) were preceded by a comparable drop.
