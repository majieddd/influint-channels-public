retention · 2

Contested retention · checked across 6 channels
Announcing the end of a video is what causes the terminal retention cliff - viewers leave the moment they perceive the core content is finished, so the transition to the next video must happen before any wrap-up signal.
Source: Sean Cannell
What would settle it

Which objective is being optimised — SESSION continuation (next-video click-through and watch time in this sitting) or REPEAT viewership (whether this viewer opens the channel's next upload)? These pull opposite ways at the outro, and each source is silently optimising a different one. The store's own 4214845e0b40 (Joos: under-delivering on a teased reward drives dopamine below baseline and suppresses willingness to wait in FUTURE videos) says the cost of the hand-off lands on later uploads, which is exactly the metric the retention curve cannot see. Restated: (a) optimising session watch time → hand off before the wrap-up signal; (b) optimising return rate on future uploads → resolve cleanly, and only then offer the next video. UNTESTABLE IN OUR DATA — we hold no next-video click-through or per-viewer return data.

Evidence and caveats →

Tested: A terminal cliff is real and large in 6/6 channels. Final-10% loss (median): Channel G 35.3%, Channel C 28.1%, Channel D 23.0%, Channel F 22.4%, Channel E 14.1%, Channel A 9.4%. The matched mid-video 10% band loses far less: Channel C 1.1%, Channel F 3.0%, Channel G 2.2%, Channel E 2.0%, Channel D 10.6%, Channel A 9.2%. Per video, final-10% loss exceeds mid-10% loss in 97 of 119 cases. Median hazard by band is U-shaped: highest at 1-5%, ~0 through 46-90%, then rising again at 91-100%.

Caveat: The cliff exists; the stated CAUSE does not follow from it. We have no labelling of where a wrap-up signal occurs, so we cannot separate 'the creator announced the end' from the trivially expected fact that people stop watching when a video is over, from end-cards, from outros, or from the curve's own end artefact. The 3.7x spread between Channel A (9.4%) and Channel G (35.3%) shows the cliff size is not fixed, which is at least consistent with an editorial cause — but that is a hint, not a test.

Most viewers will stop watching your video as soon as they realize the core content is done, so your only chance to keep them on your channel is to quickly transition them to other videos.

analytics · 4

Contested analytics · checked across 6 channels
A video with high retention but low views has a PACKAGING problem, not a content problem — the retention proves the video can hold a wider audience than the packaging reached, so it is the prime repackaging candidate.
Source: internal strategy session
What would settle it

Is the video's retention high in ABSOLUTE terms, or high as a RESIDUAL — above what the channel's own retention-versus-views relationship predicts at that view count? Only the residual carries information; the absolute number is confounded by exactly the mechanism claim B names. OUR DATA SETTLES THIS: rank correlation between retention-at-50% and first-28d views is -0.45 (Channel C), -0.51 (Channel D), -0.85 (Channel E), -0.47 (Channel A), -0.89 (Channel F), +0.07 (Channel G) — negative on 5 of 6 channels, strongly so on the two small-n maker channels. The repackaging rule must therefore be re-specified as 'retention above the channel's fitted expectation at that view count', and the existing verdict table in our own pipeline should be checked against this.

Evidence and caveats →

Tested: The quadrant is the DEFAULT, not a diagnosis: among below-median-view videos, the share that also sit above median APV is Channel C 60%, Channel D 60%, Channel A 73%, Channel F 80%, Channel G 80%, Channel E 100%. This is forced by the underlying negative views/retention correlation (pooled -0.54) — low-view videos are ALSO the high-retention ones almost by construction, so the rule flags 60-100% of every channel's underperformers and discriminates almost nothing.

Caveat: Two separate problems. First, no repackage outcomes exist in our data, so the causal claim ('retention proves it can hold a wider audience') is untestable — retention was measured on the narrow audience it actually reached, and there is no evidence it would survive a wider one; the negative views/retention correlation is direct evidence against exactly that extrapolation. Second, even as a screening heuristic the rule barely narrows the field. The team's existing 'repackage or double down' verdict table, which ranks retention-at-50% AGAINST first-28d views rather than thresholding both, is the sounder version of this idea.

Contested analytics
Despite CTR decaying with reach, first-hour and first-24-hour CTR is strongly correlated with long-term video performance on established channels — so early CTR is the usable predictive signal even though lifetime CTR is not.
Source: Paddy Galloway
What would settle it

Is day-one CTR read as an ABSOLUTE number or as a residual against the SAME channel's own prior day-one CTRs, and is that channel's day-one audience compositionally stable? Paddy's claim is scoped to established channels, where day one is a near-constant subscriber-heavy panel — that stability is what makes the comparison legitimate. Joos's counterexample is impression-accumulation rate varying per video, which corrupts absolute readings. Note the store already carries a partial carve-out from Joos himself (4f18d60d2b00: on news and patch-update channels day-one behaviour genuinely does carry signal).
