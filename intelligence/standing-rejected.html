packaging · 1

Rejected packaging · checked across 6 channels
Click-through rate is worth roughly 80% of the value of a successful video and retention the remaining 20%; a video with excellent retention and weak packaging will simply never accumulate impressions, so packaging must be solved before content quality.
Source: Ryan Trahan
What would settle it

Is retention being treated as a CAUSE of reach or as a SYMPTOM of audience narrowness? Our own data forces this question: retention-at-50% is NEGATIVELY rank-correlated with first-28d views on five of six channels we hold curves for (Channel C -0.45, Channel D -0.51, Channel E -0.85, Channel A -0.47, Channel F -0.89; Channel G +0.07). The videos that hold best are systematically the ones that reached fewest people. That is exactly what the the team audits describe from the other side (a85bd294e8c3, cc661b69dba9): reach expands the impression pool faster than click rate or hold rate can survive it, so a top video's low CTR and low retention are marks of broad distribution, not of weak packaging. The correct scoping is therefore: retention is a FLOOR (below the platform's bar, no packaging fixes anything — B is right), not a LEVER (above that floor, more retention buys no additional reach and packaging carries the outcome — A is right). Note our data cannot test the CTR half of either claim; the brief states CTR is not exposed by the API we use.

Reviewed by the team’s strategist

Click-Through-Rate when compared to similar videos are good, as this is highly influenced by two things: Your channel and video's ability to create Satisfied Watchtime Per Impression, which gives more confidence to the algorithm to push it. Then it influenced by the idea's ability to applicable to relative audiences whilst getting them to click. Its like 2 parts in this particular matter as you need to be eye-catching/familiar/easily understood FIRST in order to grab attention, then after, make the 2nd layer of understanding focus on deepening the interest through curiosity gap/novelty/missing info that really makes the viewer click on the video to find out next steps/more about the presented situation. Lastly, when tracking overtime, CTR can SEEM to go up overall on the channel, but if impressions have lowered faster than views overtime without MoM true impressions growth, this is a symptom of reduced reach as your Total Addressable Market Reach is naturally lower. So while CTR is important, it's not end all be all and comes with a lot of caveats

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: Retention-at-50% vs first-28d views: Channel F -0.89 (n=10, p<0.001), Channel E -0.85 (n=9, p<0.001), Channel D -0.51 (n=30, p=0.002), Channel A -0.47 (n=30, p=0.005), Channel C -0.45 (n=30, p=0.008), Channel G +0.07 (n=10, p=0.85). Controlling duration barely moves them (-0.88, -0.85, -0.50, -0.42, -0.42, +0.01). Duration-free clock marks agree: at 60 seconds the correlations are -0.93, -0.23, -0.16, -0.33, -0.19, -0.66. In 5 of 6 channels retention buys nothing and predicts LESS reach.

Caveat: This supports the ORDERING (packaging dominates retention as a driver of views) but NOT the 80/20 numbers, which are untestable without CTR. And the correlation is causally ambiguous in a way that matters: a video that reaches a broad browse audience is watched by less-qualified viewers, so wide reach probably CAUSES lower retention rather than low retention causing wide reach. Under that reading the negative correlation is a reach artefact and says nothing about the counterfactual value of improving retention on a fixed audience. What it does establish firmly is that within these channels retention is not a route to views.

retention · 1

Rejected retention · Maker / build · checked across 4 channels
Build videos lose a visible chunk of the audience in the FINAL 10%, after the product is tested. Adding a 'good problem' or mini-arc after the test — a new stretch goal introduced once the thing works — holds that tail.
Source: internal strategy session
What would settle it

Does the video's PROMISE resolve before its runtime does? Joos's rule assumes the open loop is still live in the back half, so late leavers are self-selected tolerant viewers. A build video's test is a terminal payoff — everything after it is post-resolution, so the late drop is caused by the structure, not by tolerance. Restated: (a) where the loop is still live at 75%, back-half analysis is low-yield; (b) where the payoff has already landed, the post-payoff span is a distinct structural defect and worth a dedicated fix. Our own curves lose 9-13 percentage points between the 75% mark and the end — 20-30% of the surviving audience — so the tail is not negligible in absolute terms.

Reviewed by the team’s strategist

an unexpected mini-problem or challenge to push it that opens up the playing field for the viewer to look forward if the challenge will be completed and make the final frame as close to the end of the result as possible

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: maker-build (n=59, 4 channels) median final-10% loss 27.2% vs a matched mid-video band of 1.9%. Per maker channel, videos losing >=15% in the final 10%: Channel G 10/10, Channel C 22/30, Channel F 6/10, Channel E 3/9. Controls: motorcycle-travel 23.0% median (22/30 videos >=15%), gaming 9.4% median (6/30).

Caveat: The phenomenon is confirmed for build content but is NOT specific to it — motorcycle-travel shows nearly the same tail loss (23.0% vs 27.2%), so this is a long-form-narrative pattern rather than a build-video signature. Gaming is the real exception. Channel E (52-min videos) is the weakest maker case at 14.1%. The prescription — add a stretch goal after the test — is a fix we cannot evaluate; no video in the set is labelled as having tried it.

strategy · 2

Rejected strategy · Challenge / entertainment · checked across 12 channels
A monthly upload cadence is compatible with tens of millions of views per video when production quality carries the concept - upload frequency is not a prerequisite for algorithmic success.
Source: Derrel Eves
What would settle it

What share of this channel's views arrive through the subscription/home feed versus suggested and search? Joos's mechanism is ROUTINE — a viewer's habit decays, which only costs you views if your distribution depends on that viewer returning. Eves states the other half himself (3d7710fb7636): consistency 'matters far less once traffic comes from search or recommendations.' The second missing variable is which metric is being defended: our strategist's 7c02437e3d5d says returning/core viewers correlate with upload VOLUME, not quality, so a monthly channel structurally cannot build a large core — it buys outlier reach and forfeits the returning baseline. Ask: am I trying to raise the floor (returning share) or the ceiling (outliers)? Cadence buys the floor.

Reviewed by the team’s strategist

Kinda. In today's algorithm, you'll find a much better time posting more frequently with lower production value, as interests change rapidly, so the video will feel more relevant or timely compared to a monthly release video, of which this all banks on the kind of niche like homemaking and such would benefit greatly from lower frequency compared to gaming channels. HOWEVER, the one thing BOTH always needs for success is consistency in cadence. The more consistent, the more it programs your audience when to expect the next video. Rewrite this law accordingly please

Evidence and caveats →

Tested — supported: 8 channels publish at >=21-day median gaps. Five of them clear 900k median views: Stuff Made Here (82.1d gap, 7,908,105 median views; 4,123,856 in the age-controlled band), I did a thing (53.0d, 4,678,904; 4,131,137), Blacktail Studio (36.0d, 3,630,772; 3,570,453), William Osman (29.3d, 3,052,009), Evan and Katelyn (28.0d, 929,084; 1,050,313). The whole maker-builds >14d cadence bucket has a median-of-medians of 571,360 vs 32,719 for the 8-14d bucket.

Caveat: This is a sufficiency claim, which is cheap to satisfy — one counterexample suffices and we found five. It does NOT show slowing down helps. The named niche (challenge-entertainment) is not in our corpus; all five cases are maker-builds, where long gaps are a consequence of build time. Views are lifetime, so older catalogues are flattered; the age-controlled band reproduces the result for the three biggest, which is the reason to trust it.
