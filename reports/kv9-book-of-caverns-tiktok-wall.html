The Fifth Cavern of KV9

The wall filmed in the TikTok video is the north wall of the well chamber in the tomb of Ramesses V and VI. It carries the fifth division of the Book of Caverns. Below is every scene on it, with the hieroglyphs that can be read on the wall, a working transliteration, an English translation, and the gaps filled from the published copies of the same wall.

Video
@msesoterica, TikTok 7685135867689602317, 4 min 44 s, 1080 × 1920, posted 2026. Caption: "The ancient egyptian procedure of creating virgin births".
Place
Valley of the Kings, KV9 (Ramesses V, completed by Ramesses VI, about 1140 BC). Well chamber E, right (north) wall, plus the door reveal into the chamber and the corridor ceiling.
Text
Book of Caverns (Egyptian qrr.wt, "caverns"), fifth division: the tableau between the goddess Nut and the great ithyphallic god, with its two extra registers below, and the litany that introduces it.
Wall condition
Complete in this tomb. Piankoff's 1944 edition marks the KV9 copy of this division "complet"; the only losses are small plaster flakes and the paint on a few figures.

How to read this page. The book runs from right to left along the wall: it opens with Nut at the right end and closes with the great god at the left end, then continues in two lower registers. Every figure faces right, toward the sun god, who is not drawn but speaks in the texts. Each scene below gives the caption exactly as it stands on the wall, then the speech of Re that belongs to it.

Three labels mark how much of each text is actually legible in the video: Read on the wall for words that can be read in the frames, Partly legible in the video when the columns are visible but only some signs resolve at video resolution, and Restored from the published text where the wording comes from the hand-copies of this same wall by Champollion (1829), Lefébure (1889) and Piankoff (1944), checked against the older Osireion copy at Abydos.

Where on the wall each scene sits

Numbers give the order in which the book is read: Nut first, then the three registers right to left, then the great god, then the two lower registers. The litany (15) is not part of the tableau; its last eight lines stand at the right edge of this wall and the rest runs back through the doorway and the corridor.

How this reading was made

The frames were pulled from the 1080p stream of the video and the sharpest frame of each shot was enlarged. At that resolution the figures, the cartouches and the larger signs read cleanly; the small columns of the speeches show the sign shapes but rarely a full word. So the wall was matched sign group by sign group against the two published copies of exactly this wall: Piankoff's hand-copy of the KV9 text in the Bulletin de l'IFAO 42 (1944, plates L to LXXIX, with the tableau drawing on plate LI), and Lefébure's 1889 plates that Piankoff numbers by. Where KV9 abbreviates, the Osireion copy at Abydos (Frankfort 1933) and the Late Period copy in the tomb of Petamenophis (Werning 2018 and 2019) supply the missing words, and those places are marked. Where the video was too coarse to settle a caption, the caption was checked on the 2019 and 2025 photographs of this wall on Wikimedia Commons (R. Prazeres; Chabe01, 4032 × 3024 and 5712 × 4284 pixels), which resolve every short caption on the wall; the chips still describe what the video itself shows.

The transliteration follows the usual Egyptological conventions (ꜣ ꜥ ḥ ḫ ẖ š ṯ ḏ; "=" marks a suffix pronoun). The English is a fresh translation from the hieroglyphs. For the enigmatic (cryptographic) spellings that this book likes, the readings of Piankoff are followed and flagged as such. Names of deities are given in transliteration first, then in a conventional English form.

The scenes, in the order the book is read

Scene 1 · right end of the wall

Nut, "the Mysterious One", lifting the sun

A goddess as tall as three registers stands at the right end of the wall with both arms raised. On one palm stands the sun child; the other lifts a disc. Before her are the scarab with a disc, a ram, and a ram-headed god standing on one disc and raising another; behind her four crocodiles carry a disc, a wedjat eye, a scarab and a ram's head. Two human-headed serpents rear at her sides, and two arms reach out from her feet toward the little figures. She is the mother in whom the sun will be reborn; the small figures are the forms the sun takes on its way to morning.

Transliteration
ḥtp Rꜥ m ḏw imnty r wḏ-mdw n imy.w tꜣ · wbn=f m ḏw iꜣbty r sḫr Wnty, Ꜥꜣpp, bin-ir.t (enigmatic: šp "the blind one") · wnn=f m sḫr pn ḥr ꜥ.wy tn štꜣ.t-irw, m kk.w=sn m sḏ.t iwt.t ꜥr nṯr.w · Ḫpꜣy
Translation
Re comes to rest in the western mountain to give orders to those who are in the earth. He rises in the eastern mountain to fell Wenty, Apophis, the evil-eyed (the enigmatic spelling says: "the blind one"). Scene 2 · first register, right

Osiris adoring, and the four human-headed serpents

A bearded god with a disc over his head walks toward the little ram-headed figure on Nut's palm with his arms raised in adoration. Behind him four serpents with human heads stand upright on their tails. These are the first inhabitants of the cavern to be woken by the passing sun.

Scene 5 · second register, right

Anubis, the four falcon-headed mummies, and the coffer with Atum's staff

Four falcon-headed mummies stand in a row under a disc. Behind them Anubis, jackal-headed, walks with his arms lowered, and behind him a coffer holds a tall staff crowned by a disc. The video describes the mummies as "sperm that turn into the four Horus or Ra"; their captions give them as forms of Horus that are the sun in its four names.

Scene 6 · second register, left

Four goddesses in their coffers around the disc

Four yellow coffers, two above and two below, each holding a woman lying on her back, with a large red disc between them. Three short horizontal lines run above, between and below the coffers, and six columns of Re's speech follow. The video calls them "four priestesses ready to be implanted"; the texts call them goddesses of the Duat whom Re wakes and lets breathe.

Transliteration

Scene 8 · third register, centre

The cauldron of heads and hearts

Two arms rise out of the ground and hold a great crescent-shaped cauldron in which four heads and four hearts are drawn. Beside it a cobra-headed god kneels and stretches a stick toward it. Three cryptographic columns are written between the arms. The video reads the heads as an "offering"; the texts say whose heads they are and why they burn.

What the video says, and what the wall says

0:12 "Ramses the Sixth tomb, which shows the process of necromancy rituals to resurrect Osiris and use his semen to implant into the Vestal priestess of the Isis temples to give birth to the demigods."
The wall carries the fifth division of the Book of Caverns, a composition about the sun god's night journey that was carved in royal tombs from Merenptah to Ramesses IX. Every text on it is either a caption ("this god is in this manner …") or a speech of Re to the gods and punishers in the cavern. No text mentions a priestess, insemination, semen (Egyptian mtw.t) or a human birth. The only birth on the wall is in the litany: "Tatenen gives you birth in the Duat", said of the sun and then of the dead king. "Vestal" is a Roman office; Egyptian temples of Isis had none.
0:41 "There is no photo available anywhere of this wall."
This is one of the most published walls in the tomb. It was copied by Champollion in 1829 (Notices descriptives II, 523–529), drawn by Lefébure in 1889 (Les Hypogées royaux II, pl. 38–41), redrawn with all its texts by Piankoff in 1944 (BIFAO 42, pl. L–LXXIX), photographed for Piankoff and Rambova's 1954 Bollingen volume, and photographed again by the Theban Mapping Project (the image reproduced below), by several Wikimedia Commons contributors (2019, 2022, 2025), and by Werning for his 2011 synoptic edition.
1:31 "Normally everything is written right to left, but this story for some reason is told left to right."
Egyptian is written in whichever direction the figures face, and here every figure faces right. The book is read from the right end (Nut) to the left end (the great god), then along the two lower registers, so it is right to left. Within each column the signs also face right and are read top to bottom.
1:46 "I am not sure if these two are children or if they are prisoners of war … he believes they are children."
They are children, and the captions name them: Ḥwn-Rꜥ, "Youth of Re", and Nnw-n-Rꜥ, "Nun-child of Re", the sun in its child forms lying in a coffer. The crowned mummy beside them is "the two who clothe Osiris".
2:02 "These two are taking the semen from the leg of Osiris."
The two bending figures are captioned "body of Atum, living of words" and "body of Khepri, living of words"; the god between them is Tatenen. They hold his arms, not a leg. Re's speech to them says: "O you who stand, who set the gods upright and shape their forms … rise in your forms, rejoice when you see my disc."
2:21 "It does talk about how they take the semen and place it into wool because air cannot come through the wool."
No text on the wall says this. The only "wrapping" words are ḥbs "to clothe, wrap" (of a mummy) and ḏbꜣ.t "coffer, sarcophagus"; the breathing on the wall is Re letting the goddesses' throats breathe ("I have let your throats breathe while I light you").
2:36 "Here below we see four priestess ready to be implanted with this that came from Osiris."
The four lying women are captioned "these are in this manner in their coffers, in the two shrines; Re sails between them and speaks to them", and Re addresses them as "goddesses, these goddesses of the Duat who are in their coffers". They are dead goddesses in coffins whom the passing sun wakes for a moment.
2:46 "Above here we see four, these are sperm with the face … underneath this we see the four sperm turn into the four, what looks like Horus or Ra."
The four upright shapes with heads are human-headed serpents, named "the Weeper", "He who came forth from Re" and "Flesh that is in Re". The four falcon-headed mummies are captioned as Horus forms of the sun: Horus-the-wrapped (Re), Horus-the-hidden (Atum), Horus-veiled-of-face (Khepri), and Horus-who-came-forth-from-the-eldest. Nothing on the wall is a spermatozoon; the Egyptians drew semen as a stream of drops, not as a tadpole.
3:07 "This is going to be followed by a beheading of four people, which look similar to the two children above … prisoners of war or slaves … an offering to the gods, probably something that will be burnt."
The four headless, upside-down figures are captioned in the parallel copy as "the enemies of this Re, handed over to these serpents of flame". Their heads and hearts are in the next cauldron, which Re describes as the one "to which are reckoned the heads and the hearts of my enemies who rebelled against me". The cobras Wepset and Neseret heat it. It is the punishment of the damned, not a sacrifice.
3:34 "These are the heads of the four that were beheaded and the heads of the four sperm."
The cauldron holds four heads and four hearts, which Re names ("the heads and the hearts of my enemies"). The third cauldron, at the bottom of the wall, holds the shadows (drawn as sunshades), the bas (drawn as birds) and the flesh of the same enemies.
3:50 "The Romans came in afterwards into this tomb, so they tried to take the sarcophagus of Ramses the 6th, and they destroyed much of this wall."
The tomb was open in antiquity and its visitors left about a thousand Greek and Latin graffiti, but they did not carve this wall. The sarcophagus was broken by tomb robbers well before Roman times, in the late New Kingdom; the face of its lid is in the British Museum (EA 140), and the box was reassembled in the burial chamber in 2004. The chamber E wall itself is essentially complete: Piankoff describes the KV9 copy of this division as "complete, somewhat abridged in the texts".
4:12 "They were implanted on Easter to give births on December 25th … It came from Egypt, traveled to Greece and went to Rome and to Israel."
Nothing on the wall concerns a calendar date. The Book of Caverns describes a single night of the sun, from sunset to sunrise, and the only "rebirth" it promises is the sun's at dawn and, by the added litany, the king's with it.
