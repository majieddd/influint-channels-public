Thumbnail directionOne clear taskFamiliar collage, larger project or room+

Hannah's originals make a multi-task home day recognizable. The proposal is a clearer hierarchy: one larger verified task or room, two or three supporting photos, and a short headline. Organization stays a practical collage; a completed makeover can lead with its actual reveal.

  • Original photos, real room finishes and the creator's recognizable presence anchor the concepts.
  • Novelty comes from the task or material: LEGO setup, stone wall, wood ceiling, storage spaces, wallpaper repair or hot tub.
  • Progress episodes show progress. Final-reveal episodes can show the completed room. A new synthetic before room would add an unsupported claim.
  • After images are AI reconstructions for review. Match faces, room geometry, furnishings and project states to source footage before a creator-channel test.

Both sides retain the same public views, upload age and duration. Thumbnail concepts and title changes have no measured performance outcome.

What to change first

Make the actual task the lead. Keep the familiar home-video style, then give the episode a concrete point of interest: a closet reset, LEGO setup, stone wall or hot-tub restoration. Use a larger verified task or room in the collage so the picture supports the title.

Give progress and reveals different promises. A beam installation is a project; a completed bathroom is a reveal. Episode numbers belong in the playlist and description when a standalone title can explain the work. Preserve the original as the test control.

Start where retention supports a test. Eight first tests have clear tasks or standalone payoffs. Eleven core candidates follow, including seasonal and progress episodes. The three travel videos need a separate demand check.
