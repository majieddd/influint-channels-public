The knowledge base's warning that a video which misrepresents the channel delivers subscribers who want something you do not make is the reason the IRL content belongs on the separate channel that already exists, and the reason the bridge videos in the second family are the only ones that should touch the main channel.

Claims

The knowledge-base claims the cards lean on

Standing as the intelligence pages record it. A law is signed; a warning is a failure mode someone has seen; a theory is unmeasured; measured means the repo's own probes; observed means a count made on this page.

law Three convergent examplesThe more independent examples you find sharing the same angle of expectation, the more likely it is a repeatable format rather than one creator's win. Around three convergent examples is usually enough to act on.
law One winner isn't a formatA single winner does not establish a format: the win may belong to the subject, the guest, a timing event or one package. Repetition is what proves it.
law Packaging outweighs retentionPackaging outweighs retention as a driver of views by a very large margin; a high-retention video with weak packaging can be dwarfed by a low-retention video with strong packaging.
warning Announcement videos are weakestAnnouncement, promo and channel-update videos are the weakest format on a channel by views and watch time, unless the creator's life is itself the product.
warning Life as the productThe announcement penalty depends on whether the creator's life is itself the product: on Her2Wheels a channel update posted the channel's highest CTR.
warning Compilations underperformAftermath and compilation content, the cleanup after the interesting part or a recut of existing footage, underperforms with the existing audience.
warning Match the scaleAn outlier score alone is not sufficient to green-light an idea: TAM must match the channel's current scale.
warning Format without variablesBranding repeats while episodes contain no meaningfully different inputs or results: the audience contract survives but the uncertainty that justified watching does not.
warning Same job or no adjacencyAn adjacency is only valid when the two audiences perform the same verb-object job; sharing a noun or a demographic is a false adjacency.
warning Vector must change eventsA broadly legible object or status marker only works when it materially changes the events or payoff; bolted on, it is empty packaging.
warning Stakes must be credibleFake consequences are discounted by viewers, while the threat of embarrassment or visible failure is sufficient.
warning Stay unscriptedFabricated obstacles trigger a credibility failure and unscripted content that plants one can be caught on a continuity error.
observed Repeats decayed hereSecond runs of the same premise read 0.21x to 0.65x on Cash Marco and Nico Polo where the first run read 1.4x to 3.0x. Observed on this page, not a knowledge-base claim.
measured Repeats don't wear outThe repo tested whether repeating a shape wears it out, on 15 channels. It did not. The action lists on both Nizarisaqt pages rest on it.
theory The swap testTake the source channel's outliers and ask whether the concept keeps its novelty and humour with your subject substituted in; formats rooted in specifically human relationships fail it for non-players.
theory Cheap formats compoundProduction complexity does not correlate with views, and simple formats outperform partly because they are cheap enough to repeat.
theory Three to five escalating roundsSegment-based videos hold best at three to five rounds with escalating lengths; one round is too thin and many rounds fail without a culminating payoff.
theory Memorable through contrastA creator becomes memorable through contrast with the people around them; casting friends with the same personality erases the creator's distinctiveness.
theory Money is never the motiveMoney is never a motivation by itself; the audience resonates once it is tied to a human need. Last-to-leave money shapes read 0.85x to 0.86x on MrBeast Gaming and Stokes Twins.
theory A serialised worldA channel should be run as a serialised world with recurring characters and a season-level arc; the arc is what makes viewers return rather than merely click.
theory Countdowns holdOn-screen countdown timers and explicit time limits hold retention through otherwise weak visual stretches and encode a promise of something at zero.
law Familiar plus mysteriousTitles that expose a knowledge gap in something the viewer already has a relationship with convert well because no new interest has to be built.
warning Curiosity without importanceA clearly stated unknown whose answer is trivial will click but not satisfy.
warning Work needs stakesContent organised around succeeding at a work task only holds when the occupation is heroic; otherwise the work must be backdrop for personal stakes.
observed Cast carries the shapeThe same party-game shapes read 2.1x to 7.9x on Cash Marco with its cast and 1.0x on Nico Polo with two people; the hate-comments video tripled when a second person read them. Observed on this page.
measured Longer holds less hereOn MoreNizarisaqt the share still watching at halfway falls with runtime (r = -0.75, n = 40); the repo's action is to cut uploads over about twenty minutes.

Limits

What this page cannot say

  • Lifts on Cash Marco and Nico Polo are lifetime views divided by a lifetime median. The knowledge base's law says the fair comparison is each video's first 28 days, which no public source gives; older uploads are flattered and the newest are undercounted.
  • Exact publish dates for the launch-era uploads could not be read: YouTube rate-limited the watch pages on the day of the crawl. Eras are read from the title sequence and the fifteen dated entries in each channel feed.
  • The one-in-three conversion figure rests on two channels and today's subscriber counts. The Roblox channels were smaller when the IRL channels launched, so the launch-time conversion was higher than 0.33 and is unknown.
  • Projections assume the IRL channel's typical upload lands where the main channel's does. The IRL channel has 76K subscribers; its first uploads will land below the base until the audience moves, and the range is the main channel's spread, not the IRL channel's.
  • No retention, click-through or viewer split exists for any IRL channel in the set. Everything about what happens inside these videos is inherited from MoreNizarisaqt's measured curves and the knowledge base's theories.
  • Format classification is by title. A video's shape is what its title claims, which is the package, not the content.
  • The niche-list medians are the latest thirty uploads, which is a week for a daily channel and a year for a slow one; the knowledge base's cadence warning applies.
  • The per-channel "views per upload" figures are over uploads aged 28 to 360 days at the crawl. Only MoreNizarisaqt's are first-28-day views; the others are lifetime views, so a channel whose uploads keep accruing (Nico Polo's do) is flattered against one whose uploads front-load. Cash Marco has no upload inside the window, so its row carries lifetime figures over all 145 uploads.

Method

How the figures were made

Catalogues were read from the public YouTube pages of Cash Marco (UCCbsKLn-kAJR6b_dXtfJj2Q), Nico Polo (UCgmU4jG1sSkpqfZ7h9_gDWA), the Nizarisaqt IRL channel (UCyaqVl5F2E8G6MY-oFyrlEA) and the twenty-two handles on the supplied list on 2026-09-09: titles, lifetime view counts, runtimes and relative upload age, plus the dated channel feeds. Nizarisaqt's and MoreNizarisaqt's figures are the repo's own: outlier scores, the Studio pull on MoreNizarisaqt, the action lists and the format library on the two channel pages. Lift is views divided by the channel's median (lifetime median for the two IRL channels, median of the latest thirty for the niche list, the repo's first-28-day outlier score for Nizar's channels). A shape's lift is the median of its uploads' lifts. An idea's projection is the main channel's median (265K) times the median lift of the uploads cited on its card, with the main channel's quartile spread (0.64x to 1.40x) as the range, which is the arithmetic the channel pages' own idea cards use. Channel profiles (avatar, subscribers, every long-form upload back to 360 days with its lifetime views) were crawled from the public channel pages on 2026-09-10; the evidence thumbnails under Why this idea are the uploads' own, stored beside this page. The 100 generated concept thumbnails were added on 2026-09-11 using the supplied thumbnail guides and the public intelligence snapshot. The ideas and the prose were written by Claude on a human brief; the figures were computed, none typed. The knowledge base's warning that an AI suggestion is never demand evidence applies to all hundred.
