Estimate reachable demand

  1. List current winners for the exact viewer promise across independent Sims and adjacent life-simulation creators.
  2. Compare recent uploads of similar age and normalize against each creator’s own baseline. Record both typical and exceptional results.
  3. Look for repeatable demand across creators. A rival’s biggest lifetime hit is neither an audience count nor a hard ceiling for Gonz.

Measure the cost of widening

  1. Publish an adjacent story between recognizable core uploads. Keep research depth and storytelling style consistent.
  2. Compare engaged views and new versus returning viewers over equal age windows, then inspect the next core upload.
  3. Expand only after the adjacent idea wins more than once without repeatedly weakening core-viewer response. A total-view increase alone does not establish that tradeoff.

Example premise to test: a Sims family history packaged around an understandable mystery, inheritance or feud, with enough Sims context to remain useful to current viewers. This is an editorial proposal, not an already-measured winning title.

The system’s claim that broader packaging costs no existing audience remains Theory (0e38b7a31735). The current public catalogue cannot measure what Gonz lost with returning viewers. No personal expansion story or quantified cost is documented here.

03

Structure the deal around the viewing experience.

Original question · mynameisgonz · sheet row 4
How do you price and structure brand deals so they don't hurt retention? Do you tie sponsor placement to a specific timestamp, and have you ever turned a deal down because of what it would do to the video's performance?
Open the source sheet
Insufficient evidence for a universal timestamp

Sell a relevant integration with an agreed story beat, scope and measurement window. No price or timestamp can guarantee that a sponsor read will preserve retention.

Price the deliverable

Quoted fee = production and integration fee + usage rights + exclusivity + additional deliverables

Set the base from production effort, audience fit and comparable deals. If using a CPM quote, define the forecast count, metric, window and any make-good terms explicitly. Keep usage rights, paid-media reuse and exclusivity separately priced so extra obligations do not silently expand the creative brief.

The published record provides no Gonz rate card, deal history or sponsor-conversion data. It cannot produce a defensible dollar quote. These are negotiation recommendations, not a valuation inferred from retention.

The system labels this Candidate Law but explicitly says it is inside the noise. The sample contains flagged moments measured over fixed 30-second windows, so clean sponsor segments can be absent. Repeated checks of the same channel are not extra independent channels.

Write these creative terms into the brief

  1. Placement: an agreed section after the video has established its promise, with the exact edit chosen around a natural transition. Negotiate flexibility rather than accepting a supposedly optimal timestamp.
  2. Integration: a relevant demonstration or concise explanation, an agreed maximum read length, and a clean return to the unresolved story. Keep the sponsorship clear when the segment begins; this evidence does not justify hiding or delaying disclosure.
  3. Review: agree the factual claims, creative approvals and revision scope in advance. Treat product relevance as a hypothesis to test, not a retention guarantee.
  4. Measurement: compare the drop into the read, the lowest point during it and the return after it against similar moments in comparable videos. A V-shaped recovery may be people skipping ahead; it does not erase the earlier loss.

For view-based deals: specify whether “views” means the newer first-frame count or engaged views. YouTube says the definition changed on 24 August 2026 while recommendations and most analytics stayed anchored to engaged views. See the Influint view-count study and YouTube’s explanation.

When to decline: when the required creative breaks the video’s promise, the product does not fit the audience, or the rights and revisions exceed the fee. This is a decision rule for the next offer. Whether the speaker has actually rejected a deal, and its measured effect, needs their own account.

The test compares titles of 3–9 words with titles outside that band, using age-adjusted first-28-day views. Each side needs at least 15 uploads. The practical threshold is 20% of the pooled median. This observational result does not prove equivalence or that shortening an individual title has no effect.

What changed the answer?

The system’s current plotted measurement record, not a story about an unseen algorithm update. It contains exceptions, and its source sentence still carries older counts. This study counts the latest plotted channel readings once each rather than repeating the historical headline.

For Gonz, retain the information that makes a character or mystery worth exploring. Test alternative framing with comparable content, and read CTR together with impressions and engaged viewing. Do not remove the useful subject merely to reach a word target.

Claim ee66537ecd72 · Title-length tables

Separate measurement changes from audience changes

A larger view count after the August definition change does not by itself mean distribution improved. Compare like-for-like engaged views and the corresponding impressions and retention before rewriting a strategy. A count definition and a recommendation objective are different things.

SHORT / One resolved problem

Show one useful Sims mod-setting fix. Make it work as a complete short, and name the larger problem the tutorial solves.

LINK / The matching promise

Use the Short’s Related Video link to the relevant complete tutorial. An unrelated popular video is a different destination.

LONG / Immediate continuity

YouTube says viewers can be recommended content across formats, but they often prefer different formats for different topics. A format experiment does not inherently confuse the recommendation system. Viewers still have to choose the longer experience. YouTube recommendation guidance.

If the two audiences are different

  1. Keep the promise aligned: create bridge Shorts about the subjects already working in the longer catalogue. Recapture or re-edit for vertical viewing rather than simply cropping a slow long-form section.
  2. Track the destination: use available traffic-source and audience reports to inspect arrivals and retention on the linked long video. Compare videos over equal age windows. Do not label total long-form views divided by Shorts views a conversion rate; unrelated traffic is mixed into it.
  3. Build long-form discovery on its own merits: the title, thumbnail and opening must also work for someone who never saw the Short.
  4. Separate channels only for a durable promise mismatch: if the audiences repeatedly want unrelated subjects and experiences, a separate home may clarify the offer. Do not split merely because Shorts views exceed long-form views.

  1. Unit: a produced long-form upload in the published video wall. Shorts and excluded live broadcasts are absent. Gryphi and SpringSims are capped at their 1,000 most recent stored produced uploads; they are not complete lifetime catalogues.
  2. Metric: use the published, two-decimal outlier score. The source computes lifetime views divided by the median of the eight nearest dated uploads with positive views, excluding the video itself; thin neighbourhoods fall back to the catalogue median. It reduces age and channel-growth bias, but does not equal a first-28-day comparison or eliminate accumulated-view bias.
  3. Grouping: case-insensitive title patterns, disclosed below. These are exploratory lexical cohorts, not the system’s semantic format assignments. Counts may differ from the original Formats cards. Groups can overlap and can include adjacent games; their counts must not be added together.
  4. Windows: the past year is 25 September 2025 through 25 September 2026, limited to the stored snapshot. “Latest 40” means the newest stored uploads, or the entire catalogue if fewer exist. “No matches” is missing evidence, not a zero-performance result. Small samples remain visible.
  5. Selection: illustrated examples are the highest-score matches in their stated full-catalogue title group, with ties broken by video ID. They deliberately show the upper end. The group median, sample size and time window are the evidence for the typical pattern.
  6. Independence: sponsor and title-length counts come from the latest one-dot-per-channel charts. Rechecks, duplicates, lifetime-only exclusions and transcript-only sponsor readings are not counted as independent confirmations.
  7. Limits: group definitions were chosen after reviewing the catalogues. Timing, game releases, creator involvement, subject quality, thumbnail changes and search accumulation can explain differences. There is no causal effect, niche ceiling, sponsor price or Shorts conversion estimate here.
